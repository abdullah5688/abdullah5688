## 1. How lines are produced: books, suppliers, copying, limits, moves

### Takeaway
Prop lines come from a mix of in-house or supplier models (algorithmic, often derived from team totals and spreads) and copying of bigger books. No book is a true "sharp" market maker for props the way Pinnacle and Circa are for sides and totals. Limits are low, typically a few hundred dollars, and books react to prop sharp money more slowly than they do on game lines.

### Cited Findings
- Props are set by "an algorithm or data", and books are "often slower to react for player props than they are for sides and totals". Many offshores reportedly use the same tool to set prop lines (tool not named in the snippet). — [Establish The Run, "Understanding the current ecosystem of NFL player props"](https://establishtherun.com/understanding-the-current-ecosystem-of-nfl-player-props/) (snippet only)
- Many sportsbooks copy their prop lines from bigger entities to save time. Huddle Gaming, a Las Vegas supplier, supplies prop odds to domestic and international books. — search-summary of general prop guides, including [CBS Sports prop betting guide](https://www.cbssports.com/betting/news/prop-betting) and [ESPN Chalk, "Why player props are the future"](https://www.espn.com/chalk/story/_/id/34371732/the-future-nfl-betting-why-player-props-not-point-spreads-expected-king) (snippet attribution is uncertain)
- Props are softer because "the sportsbook cannot model every player in every game as carefully as it models the point spread", and books protect themselves with smaller limits and higher juice. — same search summary (general guides, weak)
- An Unabated article argues "there are no sharp prop books" and suggests FanDuel may become a sharp market maker for player props. This is a contrarian vendor opinion. — [Unabated, "The Biggest Mistake You're Making When Betting NFL Player Props"](https://unabated.com/post/the-biggest-mistake-youre-making-when-betting-nfl-player-props)
- Pinnacle reportedly outsources its prop lines and hangs low limits on them. Pinnacle and Circa, the usual sharp references, do not prioritize props. — [AgentBets, Pinnacle prop bets guide](https://agentbets.ai/guides/pinnacle-prop-bets/); [sportsbooks.ag Reddit roundup](https://sportsbooks.ag/reddit/best-prop-betting-site-reddit/) (secondary, weak)
- Bettor-reported prop limits at Pinnacle and Bookmaker are about $250 pregame and $500+ on gameday, against six figures on game outcomes. — [sportsbooks.ag Reddit roundup](https://sportsbooks.ag/reddit/best-prop-betting-site-reddit/) (anecdotal, unverified)
- Prop limits are often "a few hundred dollars for less prominent players". A limit bet can move the market. — [Fox News / OutKick, "What is a prop bet"](https://foxnews.com/outkick-betting/what-is-a-prop-bet); [RotoWire Q&A with Rufus Peabody](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/beating-the-book-qa-with-rufus-peabody-40172)
- Rufus Peabody is described as a market maker on props: books move the line when he bets. — [BetChicago Q&A with Rufus Peabody](https://www.betchicago.com/rufus-peabody-pro-bettor-coronavirus-quarantine-qa)
- Unabated's game-line sharp reference, the "Unabated Line", is a vig-free blend of market-making books chosen sport by sport. Its "Market-Based Projections" back out a mean projection from prop lines across books, update as the market moves, and are meant for regressing a user's own projections toward the market. — [Unabated on X, MBP launch](https://x.com/UnabatedSports/status/1790377859267109170); [Unabated, "Introducing Market-Based Prop Projections"](https://unabated.com/articles/introducing-market-based-prop-projections); [BetSmart Unabated review](https://www.betsmart.co/tool-reviews/unabated)
- Main-line disagreement across US books is small. In one analysis of 8 US books, the median gap between the lowest and highest receiving-yards line was 1 yard, and 3 yards for passing yards. — [Steezanomics, "Do all sportsbooks have the same prop line?"](https://steezanomics.com/learn/do-all-sportsbooks-have-the-same-prop-line/) (vendor)
- Supplier landscape:
  - DraftKings agreed in August 2024 to buy Simplebet, an in-play micro-market supplier, for a reported $120–150M. It already held about 15% from a 2021 partnership. DraftKings also bought Sports IQ Analytics in 2024; a report said the plan was to combine Simplebet's microbetting architecture with Sports IQ's player-props capabilities. — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2024/08/29/draftkings-simplebet-acquisition-2/); [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/26597/the-profitability-piece-draftkings-to-acquire-simplebet-for-in-play-betting-boost); [Sports Handle](https://sportshandle.com/report-draftkings-simplebet-near-deal/)
  - Genius Sports is the NFL's exclusive official betting-data provider. — [Wikipedia: Genius Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genius_Sports)
  - Kambi sells a fully traded pre-match and in-play feed that includes player props. — [Tech-Insider comparison](https://tech-insider.org/igt-openbet-vs-kambi-vs-betgenius-for-sportsbook-trading-20-en-d182/) (secondary); [Kambi blog on NFL live betting](https://www.kambi.com/news-insights/resources/blog/live-betting-nfl-kambis-simon-noy-talks-sports-handle/)
  - Sportradar's Player Props API is an odds-comparison feed that aggregates US books' NFL prop prices. It is not a pricing engine. — [Sportradar developer docs](https://developer.sportradar.com/odds/reference/oc-player-props-overview)
  - OpticOdds is an odds-aggregation feed, community-reported at about $5k per month per sport. — [SportsGameOdds provider comparison](https://sportsgameodds.com/blog/comparing-odds-api-providers) (unverified)

### Inferences
- Props have no single sharp originator. The practical "sharp" signal is a consensus across books plus movement after limit bets by known market makers such as Peabody's group, rather than one book's price.
- Because so many books copy or share supplier feeds, cross-book agreement overstates how independent the information is. Five books at 64.5 may be one model plus copies.
- The likely pipeline is that suppliers and books derive player means from team totals, spreads, and usage, then set the line near the median of a simulated distribution and let limited sharp action move it. This is consistent with the sources but no book documents it publicly.

### Gaps
- I found nothing primary on FanDuel's, BetMGM's, or Circa's prop-origination process, or on Swish Analytics as a supplier. Search returned nothing for Swish.
- I found no primary industry-press source (Legal Sports Report, Sportico) on who supplies NFL pregame props to which US book.
- Opening times and opening limits by book are not documented in anything I could reach.

## 2. Market efficiency: open vs close, sharp vs soft, softest markets

### Takeaway
Props are widely believed to be less efficient than sides and totals, but that belief rests on practitioner claims, not academic tests. Lines move toward sharp money; Peabody's pattern is that the public pushes overs up late. The closing price is a noisier benchmark for props than for main markets.

### Cited Findings
- A personal write-up, "Measuring Sportsbook Accuracy: Analysis of NFL Player Props Using Line Deviations", covers 12,624 bets across DraftKings, ESPN BET, BetMGM and Pinnacle, ranking books by Brier score and correlation, and compares power vs multiplicative devigging. I could not retrieve its conclusions (fetch blocked). — [shaanchanchani.github.io](https://shaanchanchani.github.io/articles/sportsbook-props)
- I found no arXiv or SSRN paper testing NFL player-prop efficiency. The nearest academic work covers NFL sides and moneylines; for example, a Claremont thesis finds weak-form efficiency depending on how practical and sustainable the opportunities are, and notes that opportunities are "more prevalent in player prop and other markets" (as an aside, not tested). — [Claremont CMC thesis](https://scholarship.claremont.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5145&context=cmc_theses); [arXiv 2109.07581, COVID-19 and betting markets](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07581)
- Moskowitz (Journal of Finance) finds that betting prices from open to close react strongly to momentum and weakly to value signals, and that close-time prices are largely efficient in main markets. This is not a props paper. — [Moskowitz, "Asset Pricing and Sports Betting"](https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2021/08/AssetPricingandSportsBetting_JF.pdf)
- Peabody: "the public bets overs and on things happening". On Super Bowl props this lets sharps fade superstars at higher limits and inflated numbers; for example, Christian McCaffrey's receiving yards rose from 32.5 to 35.5 on over money. — [The Volume podcast, Rufus Peabody on Super Bowl props](https://www.iheart.com/podcast/1119-the-volume-78406522/episode/straight-fire-super-bowl-prop-148378416/)
- Peabody's team mostly bets unders. Books nudge lines up closer to game time to attract under action. His heuristic is overs early, unders late. — [The Ringer, "The King of Super Bowl Props, Part 2" (2022)](https://www.theringer.com/2022/9/28/23375387/gamblers-super-bowl-episode-3-rufus-peabody)
- PFF's blind-betting review found that passing-yardage unders, from 2020 to the 2022 midseason, were "the lone profitable category (+14.06 units)", and that receiving-yardage overs were hard to win long term. — [PFF 2022 midseason prop review](https://pff.com/news/nfl-betting-2022-midseason-player-prop-performance-review)
- One FootballGuys piece notes that NFL spreads and totals are among the sharpest markets, implying props are softer. — [FootballGuys "Odds and Ends"](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2023-odds-ends-wk05) (opinion)
- Prop vig is high. A median overround of 6.93% across 14,270 NBA main prop lines (October 2026 morning boards), against 4.76% for -110/-110. FanDuel is reported to price props at about -110 to -115 per side. — [Steezanomics, NBA prop vig](https://steezanomics.com/learn/how-much-does-an-nba-player-prop-cost-you/) (NBA, vendor); [AgentBets FanDuel props guide](https://agentbets.ai/guides/fanduel-prop-bets/)

### Inferences
- Open-to-close: on star props, public over money plausibly pushes the line up into kickoff, so the close may carry a public over-shade rather than being the "true" median. That matters for using the close as both a prior and a CLV benchmark (see section 4).
- The softest NFL prop markets, by practitioner consensus, are yardage props on lower-profile players, one-way or alternate markets, and props posted early in the week. Main-line QB passing yards and star WR yards are the most bet and most shaded. Evidence is anecdotal.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic open-vs-close study for NFL props, such as the share of the line move that predicts results.
- I found no audited sharp-book vs soft-book Brier or log-loss comparison for NFL props. The chanchani write-up may contain one but could not be read.

## 3. Documented biases: over/under, shading, medians, alt-line vig

### Takeaway
Across many non-audited samples, unders have hit more often than overs, about 52–55% on weekly NFL props and higher on season-long props, with the strongest effect in yardage props. The mechanism is consistent: books set lines near the median of right-skewed distributions and shade star overs toward public demand. The edge has varied by year (about 50% in 2020) and is often largely consumed by vig. Alt ladders and one-way markets carry more hidden vig the further they are from the main line.

### Cited Findings
- **Weekly NFL props:** unders hit about 55% from 2017 to 2019, but 2020 was much closer to 50%. — [PFF 2020 prop article, via search snippet](https://pff.com/news/bet-nfl-betting-2020-week-11-thursday-night-football-player-props)
- **Season-long props:** unders went 250-101-10 (71.2%) in 2017 and 94-60 (61.0%) in 2018. — [Action Network, NFL prop over/under season-long percentages](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/nfl-betting-player-props-over-unders-season-long-percentages); [Yahoo, "Betting unders on season long props is the way to go"](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/nfl-betting-betting-unders-on-season-long-props-is-the-way-to-go-172913329.html). Season-long unders also benefit from injury and absence risk, which pushes outcomes below the line; this is a different mechanism from weekly props. — [Action Network 2019 piece on injuries and unders](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/nfl-player-props-betting-strategies-injuries-over-unders-2019)
- **Season-long passing-yard unders:** 78% in 2021 and 71% in 2022. 4for4 warns of small samples (for example, only 5 QB rushing-TD props in 2021). — [4for4, "The Key to Winning Season-Long Player Props"](https://www.4for4.com/2023/preseason/key-winning-season-long-player-props)
- **Handicapper records:** one fantasy site's 2023 record had unders 58-36 (61.7%) and overs 46-41 (52.9%). These are selected picks, not blind bets. — [The Fantasy Footballers 2023 props recap](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/props/nfl-player-props-recap-strategy-takeaways-2023-dfs-pass-fantasy-football/). ETR's in-season records were 253-225 (+0.48% ROI) in 2023-24 and 228-158 (+14.09% ROI) in 2024-25. — [Establish The Run, props we bet 2024](https://establishtherun.com/nfl-player-props-we-bet-2024-season-long/)
- **Counter-evidence:** an Upside analysis of 1,170 optimizer-picked legs found unders at 54.81% and overs at 54.80%, essentially no difference, varying by market. — [Upside, "Are Unders Better Than Overs?"](https://upside.tools/research/unders-vs-overs-player-props). Another site that analyzed over 1M graded props (multi-sport, at closing lines) says blind unders lost less than blind overs, but both lose to the vig long term. — [SickFade, "Overs vs Unders"](https://sickfade.com/overs-vs-unders) (vendor; NFL-specific split not seen)
- "Unders do tend to outperform overs, but not enough to make them profitable as-is." — attributed in search results to a FootballGuys "Odds and Ends" piece ([2022 week 7](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2022-odds-ends-wk07); exact article uncertain)
- **Median vs mean:** books set lines where outcomes are about 50/50, which is the median. For right-skewed, zero-bounded stats such as yards, the mean sits above the median. Bettors who compare mean projections to median lines systematically land on overs. Unabated also reports a model case where the edge "is actually on the Under, worth about 2% at one book". — [Unabated, "The Biggest Mistake You're Making When Betting NFL Player Props"](https://unabated.com/post/the-biggest-mistake-youre-making-when-betting-nfl-player-props); [Unabated, "Profitable Prop Betting in 3 Easy Steps"](https://unabated.com/articles/profitable-prop-betting-in-3-easy-steps); [DumbMoneyPicks, Mean vs Median](https://www.dumbmoneypicks.ai/learn/props-fundamentals/mean-vs-median)
- **Contradiction worth noting:** one search summary of the median/mean sources said that using means against median lines makes "you'll almost always land on Unders". That is the reverse of the arithmetic, since mean > median implies overs. The standard and correct statement is that mean projections lead to overs. If a model lands on unders when compared against the line, it is not because it used means. — [Stokastic, Player Props for DFS Players](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/dfs-strategy/player-props-for-dfs-players); [Unabated](https://unabated.com/post/the-biggest-mistake-youre-making-when-betting-nfl-player-props)
- **Shading star overs:** several guides claim books shade star-player overs, raising the number or juicing the over, because recreational money favors overs on popular players. — [Wizard of Odds, "Player Props: Understanding the Math"](https://wizardofodds.com/article/player-props-understanding-the-math-behind-the-lines/); [DawBets](https://dawbets.com/learn/player-props-guide); [MoneyMaverick](https://moneymavericksports.com/picks/player-props-today) (weak). Peabody's interviews are the best practitioner evidence (section 2). One caveat: a sharp group may "own" a number early, so a star's line can hold despite public over action. — [ThunderBet 2026](https://thunderbet.ai/blog/player-props-trap-when-a-stars-line-wont-budge-2026/) (weak)
- **Alt ladders:** each rung adds vig. Rungs further out become one-way markets where books hide more margin. In Unabated's Ohtani ladder example, climbing rung by rung cost about $100 against fair prices. Unabated prices alt lines off its vig-free consensus line and a 10,000-simulation props simulator that gives fair prices at every point of the distribution. — [Unabated, "What REALLY Happens When You Ladder a Prop"](https://unabated.com/content/videos/what-really-happens-when-you-ladder-a-prop); [Unabated Alt Lines Calculator](https://unabated.com/tools/calculators/alt-lines-calculator); [Unabated Props Simulator](https://unabated.com/tools/core/props-simulator)

### Inferences (directly relevant to "our model picks almost all unders on star players")
- A real, modest structural under-lean exists: median-set lines, a public over bias, and shading on popular players. So a model that leans under on stars is not automatically biased. But the documented size is a few percentage points of hit rate (about 52–55% in good years, about 50% in 2020), concentrated in yardage markets, and much of it is eaten by 4.5–7% vig.
- "Almost all unders" from a model is far more extreme than the documented market effect. That points to model bias rather than edge. Likely causes:
  - Over-shrinkage of star means toward a population or positional mean. This is regression to the mean applied too hard, and it pulls stars below their true medians.
  - Comparing a median line with a model median taken from a mis-specified (too wide, or zero-inflated) distribution.
  - Using season-to-date averages that understate role changes.
  - Injury or absence probability double-counted when the book already voids or accounts for it.
  
  A shrinkage-toward-population prior systematically produces "stars under, scrubs over". The diagnostic is to check whether the model also leans over on low-usage players. If it does, the pattern is shrinkage, not market inefficiency.
- Recommended test: compare the model's picks against the close. Do star unders show positive CLV, meaning the line moved down after the pick? Peabody notes star lines tend to rise late on public money, so star unders should often get worse prices near the close. If the model's unders still show negative CLV against sharp-consensus closes and win at no better than about 52–53%, treat it as bias.

### Gaps
- I found no large, audited, NFL-specific blind over/under hit rate by season for weekly props at closing prices after 2020. PFF's 2017–2019 figure of about 55% is the most-cited number and its methodology was not visible.
- I found no study splitting under hit rate by player prominence (star vs non-star) for NFL weekly props. "Unders on stars" is supported only by practitioner testimony (Peabody) and guides.

## 4. Using the market as a prior: blending, CLV, devigging

### Takeaway
Sharp practice is to treat the devigged sharp-consensus price as the prior and regress the model toward it. Unabated builds this into its tools ("blend in MBPs to regress your numbers to the market"). The weight is set empirically from how well the model adds information beyond the market (out-of-sample log loss or CLV), not fixed a priori. CLV is the standard validation metric but is materially less reliable for props. For two-way props, power or Shin devigging is generally preferred over multiplicative; for ladders, price from a fitted distribution anchored to the devigged main line.

### Cited Findings
- **Blending tooling:** Unabated's Prop Odds Screen lets users "blend in MBPs to regress your numbers to the market and keep those edges sharp". Its stated purpose is to "regress [your] own projections toward the market to avoid model bias". — [Unabated on X](https://x.com/UnabatedSports/status/1790377859267109170); [Unabated MBP article](https://unabated.com/articles/introducing-market-based-prop-projections)
- **Theory:** a 2026 arXiv paper frames Kelly betting as Bayesian model evaluation. Market consensus probability serves as the prior, and the market-clearing price equals the bankroll-weighted belief of participants. — [arXiv 2602.09982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.09982)
- **Practical blend options** from a modeler's write-up: regress the model toward the line by a fixed percentage, add the line as a feature (warned this makes outputs that rarely differ from the line), or blend open and close models. — [BossChief, building a CBB model](https://bosschief.bet/build-sports-betting-model) (blog). Another guide suggests anchoring to average closing lines and adjusting only where the model has specific information. — [LandYourBets](https://landyourbets.com/how-to-build-a-sportsbetting-model) (blog)
- A paper shows that even a "bad" predictive model can beat the market when combined appropriately with market odds. This supports using a model as a modest adjustment to the market rather than a replacement. — [arXiv 2010.12508, "Beating the market with a bad predictive model"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12508) (title-level evidence; contents not read)
- **CLV definition:** the price obtained relative to the closing price, where the close is the best probability estimate. Track beating the close rather than win rate. — [TheLines CLV guide](https://www.thelines.com/betting/guides/closing-line-value/); [MoneylineMag](https://moneylinemag.com/how-to-build-a-sports-betting-model)
- **CLV on props:** one guide rates player-prop CLV as low reliability, citing low limits, few market makers, wide margins, and minimal sharp money. — [SharkBetting CLV explainer](https://www.sharkbetting.com/blog/closing-line-value-clv-explained) (secondary)
- **Low-limit noise:** football-data.co.uk (Buchdahl) found bets at low Pinnacle limits showed about 3x the expected value of high-limit bets (9.2% vs 3.0%) but ended with similar CLV. He reads a low limit as the book admitting its price is noisier. This covers Pinnacle markets generally, not props specifically. — [football-data.co.uk, "Pinnacle's Limit as a Confidence Meter"](https://football-data.co.uk/blog/pinnacle_limit.php)
- **Devig methods:**
  - Multiplicative/proportional divides each implied probability by the total. It ignores favorite-longshot bias. — [CRAN `implied` package vignette](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html)
  - Power raises the implied probabilities to an exponent k so they sum to 1. This removes more margin from the longshot. It can over-adjust longshots and under-adjust mid-range prices. — [CRAN `implied`](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html); [Walrusquant, devig methods compared](https://walrusquant.github.io/rust-bet-tracker/blog/devig-methods-compared)
  - Shin models a share of insider money and also removes more margin from longshots. — [CRAN `implied`](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html)
  - Buchdahl's "margin weights proportional to the odds" is from his Wisdom of the Crowds document. — [CRAN `implied`](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html)
  - In balanced two-way markets, power and Shin give nearly identical results, and all methods differ by under about 1 percentage point. — [Bet Hero, Devigging methods explained](https://betherosports.com/blog/devigging-methods-explained); [ImpliedScore](https://impliedscore.com/bookmaker-margin-removal-methods/)
  - One practitioner source says power is most accurate for props and multi-outcome markets; another says power beat proportional and matched or beat Shin on its datasets. — [Sharpery devig education](https://sharpery.vercel.app/education/devigging-methods); [ImpliedScore](https://impliedscore.com/bookmaker-margin-removal-methods/) (unverified practitioner claims)
- The `implied` R package documents a range of methods (basic/multiplicative, additive, power, Shin, odds ratio, wpo/Buchdahl, JSD) and is a good reference implementation. — [CRAN `implied`](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html)

### Inferences
- **Weighting.** A defensible approach is to fit the blend weight w in logit space, p = σ(w·logit(p_model) + (1−w)·logit(p_market)), on held-out props, minimizing log loss against outcomes or against devigged closes. For a new prop model without a long CLV track record, weights in the 0.1–0.3 range on the model are typical practitioner priors. This is my inference, not a sourced number. Increase w only where the model shows out-of-sample information beyond the market. Use separate weights by market type: yardage props are more likely to carry model edge than heavily bet star QB passing yards.
- **Prior choice.** Use a sharp-consensus devigged price, such as the median across books or Unabated-style market-making books, not a single soft book. On star props, the late close may carry public over shading, so the market prior itself may sit slightly high on stars. That is a reason a well-calibrated model could lean modestly under on stars, but only by small amounts.
- **CLV for props.** It is still the best available short-sample signal, but use it with caveats:
  - Measure against a multi-book devigged consensus close, not one book.
  - Expect noise because low limits mean closes reflect little money.
  - Prefer "line moved toward us" (a points move on the main number) combined with devigged price.
  - Supplement with long-run calibration and log loss against outcomes once there are hundreds of graded props.
  
  The Buchdahl low-limit result implies CLV may understate true EV on low-limit markets, but also that apparent EV there is noisier.
- **Devig choice.**
  - For two-way props at near-even prices (-110/-120), method choice barely matters (under 1pp). Use power or Shin as the default, and multiplicative is acceptable.
  - For lopsided two-way props (for example -250/+190, anytime TD) and alt ladders, use power, Shin, or odds-ratio rather than multiplicative or additive. Additive is worst at extremes because it can push probabilities negative.
  - For alt ladders specifically, do not devig each rung independently, since many are one-way and carry heavier margin. Instead, devig the main two-way line, fit a player-outcome distribution (for example negative binomial or gamma for yards) to that fair median, and read alt-rung fair prices off the distribution. This is how Unabated's simulator and alt-line calculator work.

### Gaps
- I found no published head-to-head of devig methods specifically on NFL player-prop outcomes. The chanchani write-up appears to compare power vs multiplicative by Brier score but was unreadable here.
- I found no sourced, quantitative rule from Unabated, Miller/Davidow, or Buchdahl for the model-vs-market weight in props. Miller and Davidow's "The Logic of Sports Betting" discusses using the market as the baseline and adjusting, but I could not retrieve the text to cite specifics.
- I found no empirical study of CLV-to-ROI correlation specifically for NFL player props.
